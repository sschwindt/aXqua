## A whole river reach in two phases: what the Isar example found (2026-10-13/14)

The user's order of 2026-10-13: "only run a hydrodynamic simulation of the isar case for now [...] the challenge of doing an openfoam simulation of that river stretch remains challenging enough if it is hydrodynamic only". The working case is `cases/isar-openfoam/` (not tracked, excluded locally in `.git/info/exclude`): case files that read the inputs of `cases/example-isar/` and their own job root in `_run/`. The first run of the example at 1 m cells (393,246 cells, 14 layers, 16 cores) needed 1.45 s per time step of 0.0013 s, 16 hours for 120 s of river. Four defects were behind it, none particular to this reach (commits `cf10190`, `4c25def`, `22c3981`).

**How the limiting cells were found, worth repeating before anyone changes numerics.** Write one step in ASCII, compute the Courant number of every cell from the face fluxes (`0.5 sum|phi| / V dt`), and list the largest with their phase, layer, 2D depth of the column and distance to each patch. The step is set by the worst 0.01 % of the cells. Three properties of the air (damping with `velocityDampingConstraint`, air ten times heavier, air a thousand times more viscous) changed nothing, because the cause was a boundary condition.

1. **The air on the outflow was not at rest** (`fields.outlet_pressure`, `reference_level`). `totalPressure 0` on the open top is `p_rgh = 0`, air at rest at any elevation, because the solver carries `p_rgh = p + rho g (z - hRef)`. The outflow prescribed `p = 0` above the stage, which leaves `rho_air g (z - hRef)`: 8 kPa at 816 m a.s.l. with `hRef` 0. All 84 cells with the largest Courant numbers were within 3 m of the outflow. Now air at rest above the stage, the water hanging on it, and `constant/hRef` at the lowest stage (removed again by a rigid-lid or leg C build in the same folder). Step 0.0013 s to 0.008 s. On munich-vsf the same term is 7 to 25 Pa, probably the "air loop" of 2026-10-04 (issue #4); not run there.
2. **The seed put water on dry ground** (`fields.initial_alpha`). The free surface of a dry 2D node is its own bed, the column bed is the terrain model: 583 m3 of water, a sixth of the reach, on 55 % of the dry columns. A column whose 2D depth is not above `wet_depth` starts dry. Fastest water after 1 s: 3.9 to 1.5 m/s.
3. **The inlets delivered 90 and 93 %.** `variableHeightFlowRateInletVelocity` lets in `Q <alpha^2>/<alpha>` of water. `flowRateInletVelocity` with `massFlowRate` and `rho rho` (one velocity, mass over the mass on the patch) delivers 99.5 to 99.8 %; the rest is the air's share of the mass on the patch. Level still floating (`variableHeightFlowRate` alpha).
4. **The first stage handed nothing to the second.** The solver saves at whole write intervals counted from the start of its run and not at its end; spin-up 2 s with results every 50 s ended without a result and stage 2 restarted from 0. `dicts.stage_write_interval` makes the end of each stage a write time (the production stage counts from `spinup_time`).

**What limits the step after that: air drafts at steep banks.** Air falls at 1.5 to 2 m/s through the dry columns beside the waterline and runs along the ground, through layers of 3 to 5 cm. The cause is the cells of a column next to a steep bank: they lean, and several of them hold a little water. The discrete hydrostatic balance of a VOF field on leaning sigma cells has such a state: for one 1.8 m cut bank of the example it gives a water fraction of 0.22 in each low cell of the bank column, 0.25 was observed. That mixture is heavier than air, slides, and drags the air. Not removed. `openfoam.max_flow_courant: 3` lets those cells cross more than one cell per step while the surface keeps `max_courant` 0.9: step 0.026 to 0.049 s, surface Courant number then 0.91, water cells at most 0.13. `pressure_correctors: 2`, `non_orthogonal_correctors: 1` gave the same volume, depths, velocities and discharges to three digits after 40 s on the 2 m lattice at 0.6 of the cost.

**Measured and not adopted (2 m lattice, 40 s):** Courant 2 at the surface and 6 for the flow (0.7 of the steps, lid leak up to 0.012 m3/s); `wet_margin` 2 m (a third fewer cells, ten times more wall contact, 1.2 % of the wall); `freeboard` 0.3 m with 6 layers (lid leak 15 times larger, 0.5 % of the discharge); `interIsoFoam` (no larger step, 1.5 times the cost per step; on the 2 m lattice its step was a third to a quarter of that of MULES, and its surface no sharper than with `interface_compression` 2: at 20 s 46 against 40 % of the water in full cells, 0.12 m thick in both); explicit MULES with three sub-cycles (`MULESCorr no`, `nAlphaSubCycles 3`: the surface exactly as smeared as with semi-implicit MULES); `layer_expansion` 3 (21 % of the water in full cells against 19 %); layers that follow the surface of the seed (4 or 5 of 8 layers from the bed to the 2D surface, the others growing towards the lid; built on 2026-10-14 and removed again: 26 and 29 % in full cells, but 1.96 and 7.2 m3/s of water left through the top, 0.8 and 3 times the discharge, carried by air that left the top at 2.1 m/s).

**What a result is worth: the surface is smeared over the depth of the water.** Measured as the share of the water volume in full cells (alpha above 0.99) and as the height between the levels where alpha falls below 0.95 and below 0.05 (`<scratchpad>/hyd/sharp.py`). At 1 m and 14 layers: 84 % in full cells in the seed, 54 % at 2 s, 43 % at 52 s, 36 to 37 % from 500 s to 1500 s, the surface over 4 cells and 0.23 m (p90 0.44 m) in columns of median depth 0.26 m. At 2 m and 8 layers: 6 to 11 % from 100 s on, the surface over 3 cells and 0.31 to 0.34 m. The bed layer is pinned at 0.10 m (2 ks) and the other layers are spread evenly up to the lid, so a column of 0.26 m holds 3.4 cells of water at 1 m and 2.4 at 2 m, and the surface crosses those layers wherever the bed slopes. Twice the usual compression (`openfoam.interface_compression: 2`, `cAlpha` 2 in the production stage, 1 in the spin-up) left 39 % in full cells and a surface of 0.12 m after 40 s at 2 m, against 19 % and 0.26 m, at a third more time steps; the wetted area came closer to the 2D one (12,492 against 13,780 m2, 2D 12,332) and the water lost through the top did not grow. At 1 m the state of 1500 s was continued with `cAlpha` 2 for 600 s on 12 ranks (`axqua-case/openfoam-calpha2`, a copy, restarted by hand; 6.5 hours): 60 % in full cells from 1600 s to 2100 s, the surface over 2 cells and 0.06 m (p90 0.19 m), 27.2 time steps per simulated second against 20.9 (26.4 to 27.9 in every 100 s). The reach averages hardly moved (2100 s against 1500 s): volume 2,239 against 2,246 m3, mean depth 0.246 against 0.230 m and velocity 0.643 against 0.635 m/s (over a wetted area of 9,117 against 9,751 m2: fewer columns hold traces), levels at the inflows and the outflow within 5 mm, against the 2D model -5.0 cm and +0.087 m/s where both are deeper than 5 cm (-5.2 cm and +0.086 m/s). Column by column the flow did move (`<scratchpad>/hyd/sealcmp.py <A> <B> <time A>:<time B>`): where both are deeper than 5 cm, depth +0.25 cm and velocity 0.000 m/s on average, but rms 1.7 cm and 0.089 m/s (max 8.6 cm and 0.94 m/s), reached within the first 100 s (rms 1.6 cm and 0.087 m/s at 1600 s) and kept, against rms 0.2 cm and 0.017 m/s between two results 100 s apart within either run. The bed takes more of the energy: bed friction 55.3 of the 61 kW against 50.7 kW, mean bed shear stress 9.7 against 8.7 Pa (55.7 kW at 1800 s, 50.4 kW at 1400 s, so not a fluctuation). And the water lost through the top nearly doubled: 0.032, 0.038, 0.039, 0.036, 0.035 and 0.032 m3/s in the six 100 s after the change (1.3 to 1.6 % of the discharge, mean 1.5 %), against 0.018 to 0.022 m3/s (mean 0.8 %) in every 100 s of the run before; at 2100 s 90 % of it through 95 faces where the air rises at 1.2 m/s (0.6 m/s before) and the top cells hold alpha of 1.8e-4 (6e-5), traces that the air carries above layers of clear air. The default stays 1: the reach averages do not depend on it, while the sharper surface costs 30 % more steps and nearly twice the loss, and moves the flow column by column five times as much as it varies within a run; which of the two is closer to the river has not been tested against measurements.

**Water leaves through the open top as traces of alpha in the air.** At 1 m it was 0.8 % of the discharge for the whole run (0.78 to 0.91 % in every 200 s, single samples up to 1.7 %): 90 % of it through 230 of 20,749 top faces above the deep channel (median depth 0.40 m below them), where the top cells hold alpha of 6e-5 and 1,090 m3/s of air leave and enter through the top (`<scratchpad>/hyd/leak.py`). At 2 m it was 0.02 % (0.012 % over the last 200 s). The report used to judge the largest sample and blamed the freeboard for it ("water reached the lid over up to 0.0 m2 [...] Read no velocity or level off this run"), and its discharge balance left the loss out and asked for a longer run. Now the leak is judged on its mean over the second half of the run (`SurfaceFreedom.lid_leak_mean`), contact of the surface with the top (`touched_lid`, the freeboard) and traces in the air (`leaking`) are told apart, and the balance counts the water that leaves through the top (`DischargeHistory.top`).

**Cost, and the two-level procedure.** The coarse model (2 m, 8 layers, 55,952 cells, `cases/isar-openfoam/isar-openfoam-2m.axq-case`) ran 3002 s in 2.3 hours on 8 cores beside another user's 16-process TELEMAC campaign (at 8 cores with that job the fine lattice needed 3.85 s per step, at 16 cores alone 0.49 s: the cores share the memory bandwidth). Its discharge balance: outflow above inflow by 26 % at 12 s, 10 % at 100 s, a second wave of 22 % at 300 to 400 s, 5 % at 1000 s, 1.15 % mean over 2000 to 2500 s, 0.6 % at 3002 s, 410 m3 drained, a time constant of about 1200 s at the end. From its view from above (`planview`, `depth-averaged.slf`) the 1 m model was built with `openfoam-build --option hotstart=...` (290,486 cells, the 3D wetted area plus 5 m): 0.49 s per step at 0.05 s. The job's seed step skips the 2D seed when the job names a hotstart. It ran 1500 s in 9.1 hours on 16 cores; another user's 16-process campaign took the other half of the computer from its second hour on. Its discharge balance swung by up to 8 % in the first 300 s and stayed within 1 % after 500 s.

**The result of the fine model at 1500 s against the 2D model on the same lattice:** wetted area 9,751 against 10,057 m2, volume 2,246 against 2,723 m3, mean depth 0.230 against 0.271 m, depth-weighted velocity 0.635 against 0.500 m/s; where both are deeper than 5 cm the 3D model is 5.2 cm shallower and 0.086 m/s faster (correlation of the velocities 0.84). Water levels at the two inflows 817.928 and 817.906 m against 817.975 and 817.934 m (5 and 3 cm lower; the coarse model was 8 cm lower). Against the coarse model it is 1.3 cm deeper and 0.048 m/s slower. Inflow 2.393 m3/s, outflow 2.377, through the top 0.020; the reach still gave off 0.2 % of the inflow from storage.

**The result of the coarse model against the 2D model at 3002 s:** wetted area 11,608 against 12,216 m2, volume 2,251 against 2,946 m3, mean depth 0.193 against 0.241 m, depth-weighted velocity 0.653 against 0.465 m/s, discharge along the reach the same. The upper side channel falls dry: in the 2D model it is fed by the exchange with the gravel bar (`gain_lose`, 65 L/s), which OpenFOAM does not have. The water loses about 61 kW between the inflows and the outflow (rho g Q times the drop of the water level, 2.6 m, within 2 % the same in all three results). The bed friction of the 3D models takes 59 kW at 2 m and 51 kW at 1 m (shear recomputed from k and U of the bed cells the way `nutkRoughWallFunction` does, times the depth-averaged velocity: 9.9 and 8.7 Pa on average, 90 and 72 kN); that of the 2D result takes 22 kW, a third (TELEMAC's law 5, `Cf = 2 kappa^2 / ln^2(11.04 h / ks)`, from its depth and velocity: 4.2 Pa, 43 kN on the lattice of the coarse model). So the 2D model loses two thirds of the energy of the reach somewhere else than in its bed friction (its turbulence model, the dissipation of its numerical scheme at riffles), where the 3D model loses most of it in the bed. For the same depth and velocity the wall function takes less than the 2D law: at the state of the coarse 3D model Nikuradse's law would give 20 Pa where the wall function gives 9.9 Pa. That reading is mine and is the user's to judge. Also worth knowing: with `roughness_constant` (Cs) 0.5 the formula of the wall function is rougher than Nikuradse's law for the same velocity at the same height (`E/Cs` = 19.6 against 30); 0.33 would match it.


## The outlet with a stage-discharge relation, and the mesh study (2026-10-17)

**`openfoam.outflow_boundary: rating-curve`** (default `level`) puts BAW's `waterLevel_alpha_prgh` (Thorenz 2024, `HydBCsForOF`, library `dicts.BAW_LIBRARY`, which the OpenFOAM installation of aXqua builds into the stack's user lib dir) on `alpha.water` and `p_rgh` of every outlet patch, `mode ratingCurveTable` with `data table ((Q h) ...)`; U stays `pressureInletOutletVelocity`, k/omega `inletOutlet`. The table is `case.rating_table`: `boundaries.stage_discharge` when it has >= 2 discharges, else derived over `RATING_SHARES` (0.1-3 x Q) by `boundaries.rating_method` and written as `rating-curve-openfoam.csv` in the case (BAW does not extrapolate). The run starts, and the mesher walls the outlet, at the curve's level at the case Q. `control_dict` adds `libs (...)` whenever `dicts.uses_rating_outlet(cfg)`, so `activate()` regenerating the controlDict keeps it. Refused with `mode: rigid-lid` and with `outlet_stage`. Several outlet patches each use their own Q with the same table (noted in the build). Verified 2026-10-17 on a scratch copy of the Isar example (2.7 m lattice, 79,282 cells, the stack at `~/.local/openfoam-sediment-v2406`): interFoam loaded the BC, ran spin-up and production for 1 s, outlet 1.41 m3/s against 1.59 in (still filling). The installer's `sediDriftFoam2Rating` (Olsen with a moving surface) is installed and deliberately not set up: the fine sediment case keeps bed and surface fixed.

**Mesh convergence of the OpenFOAM model** is `solvers/openfoam/convergence.py` (job kind `openfoam-mesh-convergence`): the plan spacing on the r=1.3 ladder around `plan_spacing(cfg)`, layers unchanged, every level built (`openfoam_dir` per level below `<postprocessing>/openfoam-mesh-convergence/`) from the same seed the executor prepares (`_pre_step`), run, and compared through `planview.write` (`depth-averaged.slf`) with `axqua.convergence.run_mesh_convergence(result_name=..., check_validity=False)` - the TELEMAC closure checks of `mesh_validity` do not apply. **Run for real on 2026-10-17** on a scratch copy of the Isar example (`end_time` 1 s, 4 MPI ranks, 7 probes from the ground truth): levels 3.51 / 2.70 / 2.08 / 1.60 m plus one automatic extension at 1.23 m (46,886 to 262,318 cells, 60 to 427 s each), the next extension at 0.95 m refused by `checkMesh` (one incorrectly oriented face pyramid) and handled as the practical refinement limit; verdict not converged on the relative change, as expected after 1 s of a filling reach - a test of the chain, not a result. That run found the decomposition bug: `backend.run(n_processors=4)` on a case built for 1 decomposed into one domain and mpirun refused ("Number of processor directories = 1 is not equal to the number of ranks = 4"); The decomposition for the ranks of a run lives in `OpenFoamRuntime`, decided per stage from one read of the case (`runtime.Layout`: number of domains, also from the `processors<N>` folder of a collated decomposition; the latest time in the processor folders and at the top level; whether the active controlDict continues from `latestTime`). **`decomposePar` carries over exactly one top-level time, the one the active controlDict starts from** (measured with v2406 on 2026-10-18), so whether a split loses anything depends on the stage: `run_stage` activates the stage first, then `_prepare` makes the case ready for it. A stage that starts afresh moves what earlier runs left into `previous-run/` (`layout.set_aside_runs`: the time folders after 0, the processor folders and `layout.RUN_PRODUCTS`, i.e. `postProcessing` whose monitors `report.read_monitors` would otherwise join to the new run's, `VTK`, `depth-averaged.slf` and the report files; a later time is where the next stage would continue from, also an old reconstructed result at the top level) and, on several ranks, splits the case anew from the start time. Moved, not deleted (review of 2026-10-18): the processor folders of a run that was not reconstructed may hold the only copy of hours of computing, and the new run has produced nothing yet; only the run before is kept, the next one replaces the folder, and `calibration._clean_template` deletes it. A stage that continues runs on results that only the processor folders hold, refuses (`SolverError`) where those were split for other ranks or the stage runs on one processor, and splits again from the latest time where the top level holds it (after `reconstructPar`). `build_case` sets them aside as well, before it writes the new case (review of 2026-10-18: set aside afterwards, a build that failed while writing left the old times and processor folders beside the new mesh; a second attempt finds nothing to move and keeps `previous-run/`), because the results of an earlier build cannot be continued on a new mesh, its processor folders hold the old mesh itself, on which a parallel stage would go on, and its depth-averaged result would be listed as one of the new case. Every question of which folders are times, which are processor folders and how the case lies on disk is answered by `solvers/openfoam/layout.py` (standard library only; `runtime` re-exports `Layout` and `processor_count`; planview, sediment, spec and the backend use `time_folders`). `describe_results` describes a result that only the processor folders hold as `Dataset.decomposed`, with the times of the first domain, and `postproc.visit._open` then sets VisIt's OpenFOAM option `Case Type` to 0 (Decomposed): VisIt 3.5.0 opens a case reconstructed by default and showed t = 0 alone for such a case, measured 2026-10-18. Nothing splits before the stages any more (the backend, the calibration pre-spin and the tracked `openfoam_run.py` scripts leave it to `run_stage`, so a run decomposes once); the public `decompose` stays for munich-vsf's script (not edited; it decomposes without its ranks and is split again by the first stage) and never splits over results only the processor folders hold (it returns None), which is what keeps that script's stage-by-stage resume (spin-up without reconstructing, production stage in a second call) working: checked with v2406 on the scratch Isar copy, the production stage continued from the spin-up at 0.1 s. All of this happens without touching the case's own file: `dicts.decomposition_for` returns None where `system/decomposeParDict` names that count (it is used as it is, with a method that may be the user's) and otherwise writes aXqua's scotch file `system/decomposeParDict-<n>`, which `decomposePar` and the parallel solver both get with `-decomposeParDict` (a parallel interFoam reads the dictionary too and refuses one that names more domains than it has ranks). Checked with OpenFOAM v2406 on a scratch copy of the Isar example (case file for 1 domain, run on 3 ranks through the backend and through the flow of the scripts, both stages, reconstructed, case file unchanged); `calibration._clean_template` strips the `decomposeParDict-<n>` files. An earlier version swapped the case's file and put it back in a `finally`, which a killed runner never reaches (review of 2026-10-17). The calibration template is the one case others decompose with its own file (HydroBayesCal copies it), so staging it calls `dicts.write_decomposition` with the config's `n_processors` before the pre-spin. The size scale of a level is `dx / plan_spacing(cfg)`, recomputed after the study for levels taken over from `level.json`, and written back into that file.
