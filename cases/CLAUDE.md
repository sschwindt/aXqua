# The example cases

Moved from the root `CLAUDE.md`; loaded when working under `cases/`.

## The fast end-to-end example (`cases/example-isar/`) and what running it found

`cases/example-isar/example-isar.axq-case` is the isar-2025 research case with `mesh.size_scale: 3.0` (26,500 nodes instead of 230,000) and own output folders; it reads its inputs from its own `user-sources/` folder, a reduced copy of the research inputs that is tracked in the repository (see "The example case is a folder that works on its own" below). Since 2026-10-15 it carries the corrected measurements again (`user-sources/ground-truth/flowtracker-2025-{11,09}.gpkg`, copied by `scripts/build_example_data.py` from the research case). Build 1 min, steady run 1.5 min (flux balance below 1e-3 after ~2240 s of 3000 s), a 12-run HydroBayesCal calibration of `zone4`/`zone5` against the 33 November verticals inside the model in 16 min, validation against the 83 September verticals 1.5 min, all on 8 cores. Its `README.md` is the step-by-step guide for the plugin. **Use it whenever a change has to be tried through the whole chain** - the research case costs a day for the same answer. It keeps the `gain_lose` block of the research case. **An exchange now needs the case to ask for it** (`boundary.exchange_regions`): the `int-*` lines are a property of the liquid-boundary layer, a layer is shared between cases, and they used to be applied whenever the layer held them - so a case that said nothing got an exchange anyway, as thin fixed-rate SOURCE REGIONS, which blow up at the first time step on this reach (rung T2 of `cases/isar-2025/test-approaches.md`, reproduced on the coarse mesh in 2 s). Without a `gain_lose` block the lines are now named in the build log and left out; `boundaries.internal_sources: true` is the explicit way back to the fixed-rate regions. The stale `isar-2025-DEB` and `-Mint` variants, which predated the lines, were removed on 2026-10-15.

**The case has two FlowTracker campaigns, and they are two flow situations.** `flowtracker/Nov25/` (24-25 Nov 2025, `Isar_Nov25.gpkg`) was measured at 2.6 m3/s in the main channel with 1.63 + 0.94 gauged upstream - the discharge of the model (2.4 m3/s, inflows 1.6 + 0.8 from the `Target flow` column, which `prescribed_flowrate` does not rescale). `flowtracker/Sep25/` (30 Sep 2025) was measured at about 5.3 m3/s (5.30 at the outlet transect, 5.32 and 5.09 upstream; 0.18 in the side channel). The example therefore **calibrates against November and validates against September** (`calibration.validation` with `inflows: {1: 0.2, 3: 5.1}`); it used to calibrate against September at 2.4 m3/s, which could not fit (-42 % after calibration). Of November's 56 verticals, 23 lie upstream of the model where the inflows were gauged; `bayescal._inside_the_model` now leaves points outside `geodata.boundary` out of the calibration CSV, because HydroBayesCal reads the nearest node with no distance limit.

**Neither campaign is reproduced, and the validation shows why that matters.** With k-epsilon (the automatic choice since 2026-11-03: filter width about 1.4 m against a depth of about 0.3 m) and `duration` 6000 s, measured 2026-11-04 on 8 cores (build 1 min, steady 2.3 min, calibration 30 min, validation 2.5 min): the imbalance stays below 0.1 % from 3370-3590 s on (one build had not reached it at 4000 s, hence 6000 s; under Spalart-Allmaras the same 3000 s run ended at 0.11 %, so the old "balances after 2200 s" no longer held either). In the 12 calibration runs the November velocity is 7-26 % low at 67-73 % of the verticals (+10 % with the lowest roughness in both zones), the depth 0.17-0.24 m against 0.28 m, and verticals 1-9 (one cross section, 0.19-0.36 m measured) are dry in every run; HydroBayesCal reports correlated depth and velocity residuals (roughness alone not identifiable), 1 of 5000 prior samples accepted, calibrated zone4 0.30 and zone5 0.60 (both upper bounds). Validated against September at 5.3 m3/s: velocity -11 %, depth -3 %, RMSE 0.32 m/s and 0.20 m, 24 % / 16 % within one measurement error, 5 verticals dry; since the 15-node rule (2026-11-04: measurement points less than 15 mesh nodes from a liquid boundary are dropped, `bayescal.away_from_liquid_boundaries`) 29 of the 83 September verticals near the outflow are left out, and the 54 others give velocity -17 %, depth +2 %, RMSE 0.22 m/s and 0.11 m, correlation 0.90 / 0.83, 33 % / 17 % within one error, 1 dry. All 33 November calibration verticals are 15 nodes or more from the boundaries, so the calibration is unchanged. Under Spalart-Allmaras (2026-10-15) it had been velocity 33-40 % low, depth 0.29-0.33 m, posterior mean zone4 0.30 / zone5 0.30, validation -21 % / +22 %. So there is a cause that is not roughness and not the positions: candidates are the DEM against the channels of November and the split among the braids. The research model at full resolution gave the same mean velocity at the September verticals as the coarse example, so the mesh is not it.

Running that chain through the same CLI the plugin calls found defects no unit test had, each now pinned by one:

- **TELEMAC-2D ends a blown-up run as a success.** `CONTROL OF LIMITS` is a "neat (programmed) stop" in `telemac2d.F`: `LIMIT VALUES TRESPASSED, TELEMAC-2D IS STOPPED`, then `CORRECT END OF RUN` and exit code 0. The job was COMPLETED with U = -1002 m/s at t = 0. `workflow.run_solver_streaming` watches the listing for the marker and `_fail_on_limit_stop` gives the process return code 2 (TELEMAC's own code for a controlled error stop) plus `proc.stop_reason`, read from the listing by `sortie.find_limit_stops` - **from the per-processor `_p0000N` listings too**, because in a parallel run only the subdomain that owns the node names it. The backend raises a `SolverError` carrying quantity, value, coordinates and simulated time (`tests/test_limit_stop.py`).
- **A point layer as ground truth** (`kind: points`): column names with a unit in brackets (`v(x) [m/s]`, `Total Depth [m]`) are canonicalised, several readings on one vertical are reduced by the same `profile` rules as a FlowTracker workbook (`select_point_profiles`, grouped by position to 1 cm or by `group_key`), and a `z` column of zeros is a placeholder for "no elevations", not a survey 816 m below the bed (`tests/test_ground_truth_points.py`).
- **`dump_config` must not write a block the case never declared.** Every optional block has a dataclass with defaults, a written block is a declared block on the next load, and so every TELEMAC case came back from a dump asking for OpenFOAM as well - in a job's frozen copy and after `axqua migrate`. `_never_asked_for` skips an undeclared block that still equals its defaults (machine fields aside, since the loader fills those from the profile).
- **A single-flow calibration rewrites the built case in place.** HydroBayesCal's TELEMAC control runs in `model_dir` and writes each tested parameter set into `friction.tbl` and the results name into `steady2d.cas` (and `user_fortran/user_rain.f` for `gainlose_kf`), so the case was left holding the *last* set tried and the next steady run used it. `bayescal.case_as_built` copies those files into `calibration-validation/case-as-built/` and restores them when the driver returns; `restore_as_built` is also called before every TELEMAC launch, because a cancelled calibration is killed and restores nothing. Jobs of one case are queued one behind the other for this reason (`jobs/workspace.py`, see `src/axqua/jobs/CLAUDE.md`).
- **A calibration reports its run number** (`hbc.RunCounter` follows the driver's `logfile.log`). Read `Running full complexity model <N>`, which counts through both phases; `... model # N` and `... after BAL # N` each restart at 1. The driver truncates and rewrites that log between its phases (it contains NUL bytes afterwards, so `grep` needs `-a`).
- **The convergence jobs wrote no report.** `mesh_convergence_study.py` and `vertical_convergence_3d.py` call `report.to_xlsx()` / `report.save()` after the study returns; the job kinds returned the report object and wrote nothing, so the verdict was in `runner.log` only - although `export_qgis_results` already listed the workbook among the results. `backend._write_study_report` now does what the scripts do. Also worth knowing: `default_probes` uses the ground-truth points only when `preprocessing/ground-truth.xlsx` exists, i.e. **after a build**; on a fresh case it silently falls back to 40 points along the centerline, which on this braided reach are mostly dry and gave "22 % not converged" where the 84 verticals give 7 %. Both studies report each run as it finishes (`on_level`, shown as `level 2/4`). **The script and the job keep the study in two different folders**: the job, the capability spec and `export_qgis_results` use `postprocessing_dir/mesh-convergence`, the per-case `mesh_convergence_study.py` uses `postprocessing_dir.parent/mesh-convergence` - so one cannot resume the other's levels. Left as found, because moving either orphans existing studies.
- **The Steady 3D tab ran a simulation nobody could find afterwards.** The `steady-3d` kind defaults to the `hydrostatic` variant, which writes `r3d-hydrostatic.slf` / `r3d-2d-hydrostatic.slf`, while the capability's `run` predicate and `export_qgis_results` looked for the non-hydrostatic `r3d.slf` only. Both now know the hydrostatic names; the depth-averaged companion is what QGIS loads (it carries U and V but no `SCALAR VELOCITY`, so it gets no velocity-styled entry). The 3D volume files (`r3d*.slf`) are listed as files, not layers: QGIS reads SELAFIN in 2D only and each *Load results* ended with "r3d.slf did not load". The plugin's option table gained a `choice` type for `variant` (the first entry carries no value and is not sent). On the example the 3D model gets **two** vertical levels; the hydrostatic run takes 19 min for 4800 s, the non-hydrostatic one 10 min for 2035 s.
- **HydroBayesCal was not on PyPI above 1.7.0 until October 2026**; since then 1.9.0 and 1.11.1 are (checked 2026-10-19), so `pip install "axqua[calibration]"` resolves from PyPI alone. **The floor is 1.11.0** (2026-11-04): 1.9.0 divides each output by its spread over the initial runs, and the verticals that are dry in all eight runs under k-epsilon gave NaN and stopped the calibration after its initial design.
- **A build after a cancelled calibration was undone by the next run** (2026-11-04). The killed calibration leaves `case-as-built/`; the next build wrote a new `steady2d.cas`, and the steady run's `restore_as_built` copied the old one over it (the old `DURATION`, with its old timestamp). `pipeline.run` now discards the copy (`bayescal.discard_as_built`; `tests/test_bayescal_staging.py`).
- **Two `FORTRAN FILE` lines** when the gain-lose routine and a turbulence routine share `user_fortran/` (2026-11-04): `write_cas` looked for the keyword before the gain-lose block added it. TELEMAC accepted the duplicate; the check now runs where the keyword is emitted (`tests/test_source_regions.py`).


## The example cases are folders that work on their own (`core/examples.py`, `axqua example`)

**"The example cases" means the user's own examples (Isar, Inn), never TELEMAC's.** The user's decision of 2026-10-10: aXqua downloads its example case, reduced to "only what is absolutely necessary and manageable via GitHub without lfs", and aXqua itself contains no external software and no installer ("otherwise aXqua gets way too big"). A question about "the example cases" was once read as TELEMAC's and cost a round, which is why the TELEMAC download is now spelled `axqua install telemac-examples` and `install/telemac_examples.py`.

**An example is `cases/<name>/`: the case file, `README.md` and `user-sources/`**, with every path in the case file relative to it, so the folder can be put anywhere (`test_an_example_reads_nothing_outside_its_folder` walks the case file and requires each path to be in the index). `scripts/build_example_data.py` builds `user-sources/` from the research case: layers and tables are copied byte for byte, and **the terrain model is cut to the outline plus 5 m and stored as float32 with DEFLATE and the floating-point predictor**. For isar: 35.0 MB -> 1.9 MB, 13 files and 5.9 MB in all against 38 MB. A rebuild writes the same bytes, so the tracked files only change when the data do. That is not a simplification of the model: `dem.clip_to_roi` cuts the terrain to the outline before anything else reads it, and from the reduced file it returns the **same 1,055,282 cells** on the same grid, each within 0.031 mm (the resolution of a float32 at 816 m). A steady run of the fetched example lands inside the scatter of four earlier runs from the original data (26,559 nodes against 26,503 to 26,572; water volume 3009 m3 against 2982 to 3010; mean velocity 0.449 against 0.449 to 0.453 m/s), which is the scatter of the mesher, not of the data.

**`cases/examples.json` is the index**: every file of every example with size and SHA-256, written by the same script (`--index`, `--check`). **An example is an explicit list** (`EXAMPLES[name]`: the case file, `documents`, `files`), not "whatever lies in the folder": results, `MODEL=` markers or a project file saved there are never published, and `test_every_tracked_file_of_an_example_is_in_the_index` keeps the list and the repository in step. A download compares each file with it and moves the folder into place only when all files are there, so a broken transfer leaves nothing that looks like an example. **Editing the guide or the case file of an example without `--index` makes every download refuse that file** - found by the headless plugin run, pinned by `test_the_index_of_this_repository_is_what_its_folders_hold`. A folder that exists is never overwritten: results may be in it.

**Where an example is read from** (`examples.sources`): `--source` / `$AXQUA_EXAMPLES`, then the `cases/` folder of a source checkout **if it holds the data** (a clone may carry the case file without them and is then passed over), then GitHub raw at `v<version>` and at `main`. The module uses the standard library only, so it works where nothing else of aXqua runs yet. In the plugin: *Example case...* on the Case Setup tab (`gui/example_dialog.py`).

**The data are tracked in this repository, by the user's decision of 2026-10-10** ("inside aXqua, these small sizes are ok"): about 2.4 MB more in a clone. Two files make that work. `.gitignore` re-includes `cases/example-isar/user-sources/**` **in its last lines**, because the last matching rule wins and both the rule for `user-sources` and the file types (`*.tif`, `*.gpkg`) come before; the research cases stay ignored. `.gitattributes` marks the example folder `-text`: a guide checked out with CRLF on Windows would no longer be the file the index describes, and a checkout used as a source would refuse it.

**The data carry their own license** (`cases/example-isar/LICENSE.md` and the verbatim `LICENSE-CC-BY-4.0.txt`, both in the index, so they travel with a download): CC BY 4.0, which is what the user asked for, "a license that excludes any liability and enables reuse upon citing aXqua". The citation line and the copyright line there (the repository's own, "2026, Sebastian Schwindt") were written by me from `pyproject.toml` and `LICENSE` and are the user's to change. The case file and the guide stay under the BSD license of aXqua.

**The example had no measurements from 2026-10-10 to 2026-10-15**, while the DGPS positions of the Isar FlowTracker campaigns were being corrected. They were restored on 2026-10-15 from the local tag `archive/example-with-measurements` (guide and case file), with the corrected layers as source and `profile: depth-average`.

**The corrected tables arrived on 2026-10-14** (Isar, Inn KB8 and KB15; four campaigns each for the Inn: Sep25, Nov25, Mar26, Aug26; three for the Isar: Sep25, Nov25, Aug26). They are point layers with one point per reading and headers with units in parentheses (`v_x (m/s)`, `Total Depth (m)`), which `ground_truth._canonical_name` reads since then. They live in `cases/<case>/user-sources/ground-truth/flowtracker/<Mon><YY>/` with a `README.md` per case that lists date, discharge and role of each campaign; `isar-2025`, `isar-openfoam` and both Inn case files read them (calibration with the campaign of the model's discharge, validation with the others; August 2026 is prepared but commented out, its discharges are unknown). What the correction changed, measured: the Isar positions are identical to the July layers (one September reading removed); KB15 September and March moved uniformly by 0.23 m, November not. The example carries them since the same day (the user's decision). The geodata of `inn-KB08-2025-hydro` exist on neither the second workstation nor the main workstation.

A source of the kind `points` reads a point layer or a CSV/Excel table with coordinates; the documentation lists the column names (`docs/usage/calibration-validation.rst`, pinned by `test_a_plain_table_with_coordinates_is_a_source_like_a_layer`).
