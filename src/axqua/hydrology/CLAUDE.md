# Hydrology mode

Moved from the root `CLAUDE.md`; loaded when working under `src/axqua/hydrology/`.

## Hydrology mode: rainfall-runoff with Green-Ampt (`axqua/hydrology/`, TELEMAC only)

**The user's order of 2026-10-24**: a sub-tab *Simulations > Hydrology*, a *Hydrology mode* check box in mesh generation, a preprocessing checkup and a data wizard. User decisions: Green-Ampt default = the Sadeghi solution recommended by de Vos et al. (2024, TUC XXX, hdl 20.500.11970/114347; the original implementation is Broich et al. 2022, hdl 20.500.11970/110848), rain uniform in space, wind AND evaporation included, the wizard downloads data itself after the national-agency hint.

**The block `hydrology` with `enabled: true` is the mode** (`config.Hydrology`, layer TELEMAC). `Config.outflow_condition` returns `hydrology.outlet_condition` in the mode, so boundary/steering/hydraulics read one property. The mode needs no discharge and no inflow line (a case without any liquid line is a closed catchment; `classify_nodes` returns all walls, `read_sortie` accepts listings without boundary fluxes); `gain_lose` in fortran mode and `prewet_depth` are refused. STEADY2D and MESH_CONVERGENCE are not configured in the mode; `Capability.HYDROLOGY` / `JobKind.HYDROLOGY_RUN` (`hydrology`) run `hydrology2d.cas`, built by the ordinary build (`pipeline._write_hydrology`; the river stage 4 moved unchanged into `_write_river_steering`).

**TELEMAC v9.1.1 ships only Travert's Green-Ampt** (`RAINFALL-RUNOFF MODEL 3`, hard-coded constant rain, no infiltration of ponded water after rain); de Vos's revision is not upstream (GitLab main, checked 2026-10-24). So aXqua generates `user_fortran/runoff_greenampt.f` (`solvers/telemac/greenampt.py`) with TELEMAC's exact interface: kernels `sadeghi` (default), `exact` (Ks(1+a/F)), `travert`; forcing (rain + evaporation, mm/h) from `FORMATTED DATA FILE 1` (`forcing.txt`); Ks [mm/h], `GA SUCTION A` = psi (theta_s - theta_i) and `GA DELTA THETA` per node as named private variables of `geometry.slf` (matched on 16 chars, `fonstr.f`), `INFILTRATED` written back. **PROSOU runs once per sub-iteration of a step**, so the routine computes from the state at the start of the step and commits when LT changes. It prints `AXQUA HYDROLOGY t rain infiltrated evaporated` [m3] at listing printouts (`hydrology/report.py` reads it with TELEMAC's FINAL BALANCE). The Python reference `hydrology/greenampt.py` is the same algorithm; TELEMAC and it agree to four digits on a flat closed basin (FE, FV, 4 cores). Sadeghi as de Vos give it infiltrates 1.4-4 % less than exact Green-Ampt under constant rain; `exact` is exact.

**Two findings that changed the plan, both measured on a synthetic V-catchment** (scratch, not tracked; the test fixture `tests/test_hydrology._catchment` is its small copy): the free outflow (4 4 4) ponds (FV) or blows up (FE, "ILL-POSED PROBLEM, ENTERING FREE VELOCITY") unless the outlet is supercritical, so **the default outlet is `normal_depth`**: TELEMAC's `STAGE-DISCHARGE CURVES` Z(Q) per outflow line from the line's DEM section (`rating.line_section`, ks of the roughness zones, else `friction.default_*`, never the lateral-wall keyword) and the slope upstream (`hydrology.outlet_slope`). And **the hydrology kernel defaults to finite volumes + `OPTION OF THE HYDROSTATIC RECONSTRUCTION : 2`** (Chen and Noelle, TELEMAC's `rain_runoff` validation): the classical reconstruction fails where h < bed step between nodes; FE left 13 % of the rain as film 30 min after the storm vs 0.5 % with FV. Equilibrium Q = iA reached to 0.01 %.

**Wind and sun**: wind through TELEMAC (`WIND`, `OPTION FOR WIND 2`, `atmosphere.txt` with components from the from-direction; `THRESHOLD DEPTH FOR WIND` default 1 m); evaporation computed by aXqua (FAO-56 ET0 x open-water factor 1.05, or hourly Penman-Monteith eq. 53, checked against FAO-56 Example 19) and taken from surface water only in the routine. **Mesh**: in the mode `mesh._hydrology_size_field` (isotropic, zone `Max Edge Length` applies isotropically, growth-limited size grid, Frontal-Delaunay), never BAMG. **Checkup** `hydrology/checks.py` (codes `axqua.hydrology.*`, anchors in `docs/troubleshooting/`, the terrain part = priority-flood wall crossings + depressions in `hydrology/terrain.py`), run by `axqua hydrology check`, by `axqua check` (without terrain) and at the start of the build (errors stop it). **Data**: `axqua hydrology info|portals|textures|rainfall|design|fetch|check` (`hydrologycli.py`); `fetch` = Open-Meteo ERA5 (event + soil moisture), SoilGrids WCS (textures -> Rawls 1983, values checked against the scan of the paper; the TUFLOW wiki copy has loam and silt loam wrong), ESA WorldCover COG (class 50), each with a provenance `<file>.json`. **Docs: the section *Hydrology* (`docs/usage/hydrology-simulations.rst`, between morphodynamics and calibration) is written to be read on its own** (the user's order of 2026-10-25: it "should take up everything one needs to understand from the preceeding sections"): a table of what differs from a river case, step by step with links to the river pages, a subsection *Case setup* that goes through the pages of the case settings for a catchment (with a table of every `hydrology` entry and its default), *Preprocessing* (the five build steps in the mode and the checkup), and *Mesh resolution, calibration and limits* (a manual r = 1.3 refinement with `mesh.size_scale: 0.77` replaces the mesh study; calibration is not yet available, so `spec._calibration_configured` is false in the mode). Its outline is pinned in `tests/test_docs_outline.py`; when a hydrology feature changes, check that table and the case-setup subsection too. The volume and pages of Fernandez-Pato et al. (2016, J. Hydrol.) could not be confirmed online and are left out of its reference.

**What the four reviews of 2026-10-25 fixed, each pinned in `tests/test_hydrology.py`**: `Config.hydrology_mode` is the one test of the mode (code that also gets stand-ins reads `getattr(cfg, "hydrology_mode", False)`); a listing without boundary fluxes passes `read_sortie` only when it says itself that its run had no liquid boundary (`THERE IS n SOLID BOUNDARIES:` without a liquid line before it in a serial run, `NUMBER OF LIQUID BOUNDARIES: 0` in a parallel one; `Sortie.liquid_boundaries`), so the run decides and not the case as built now, and a river run still gets the error; the build refuses `morphodynamics`, any `gain_lose` and `internal_sources` in the mode, because `_write_hydrology` writes none of them; a dry run launches `hydrology2d.cas`; the rain reader takes integer dates (`MESS_DATUM`, MeteoSwiss `time`) as dates in any column, years 1900 to 2100, except ten digits whose every step is a whole number of minutes (Unix seconds, which parse as `YYYYMMDDHH` from 2030 on; `YYYYMMDDHH` read as a number never steps so), `R1`/`RWS_10`/`RS` as depths in mm whatever the units and stamp settings say (end, end, and for `RS` the DWD's precipitation day from 05:50 UTC of the date to 05:50 UTC of the next, as the parameter metadata in each station archive say; 07:30 legal time before April 2001, `rainfall.dwd_day_starts`), -999 as missing; both readers keep only data rows (`rainfall._data_rows`: before the first and after the last time a row is left out only when it cannot be one with a broken time, i.e. its time has no digit or it has no number, a units row or a sum; an empty row or a repeated header inside is left out; any other row without a readable time stops the import with its value), and the weather reader interpolates a missing reading in time (`weather.fill_gaps`, the direction as an angle; warning `weather_gaps`), where -999 used to reach the evaporation and the wind; a missing value stays a dry interval of its own (`RainSeries.missing`, written empty into the canonical file so the checkup still sees it, warning `rainfall_gaps`, and a `caution` in the wizard) instead of stretching the next row over it; a design storm or an undated record clears `hydrology.rainfall_start`, and undated rain under dated weather is the warning `weather_alignment`; the size grid is cached per process by its inputs (`mesh._SIZE_GRIDS`) and the element estimate belongs to the slow checkup (`terrain=True`); `rating.ks_from_friction` is the one conversion of a friction law to ks; `steering.read_cas_values` is the one reader of steering-file keywords (`KEY : value` or `KEY = value`, comments skipped; `threed` and `cas_duration` use it). The fourth round: a time column pandas holds as dates (Excel date cells) is read as dates, not as nanoseconds (`rainfall._parse_times`, parsed once and handed to `_seconds`); a row whose time is the name of the time column is a repeated header wherever it is; ten digits whose rows lie more than a day apart as dates are Unix seconds too (a jittering logger); a DWD value covers its own period (`AGENCY_COLUMNS` carries it; `dwd_day_windows` ends each day by its own rule, so the change of April 2001 leaves 20 minutes that belong to no day) and a row such a file does not have becomes a gap (`_with_holes`); in a serial run with the listing printout off TELEMAC prints neither the boundary statement nor the balance blocks (`bilan.f` prints under the same switch), so a closed domain is never misjudged; the docstrings of the hydrology mode are unwrapped, one line per paragraph, as the user's rule asks.

**The second example, `cases/example-agrohydrology/` (2026-10-25, the user's order: "use these cases to generate one or two example cases (you may want to merge these two models into one)").** The user placed two TELEMAC models of one arable field in `cases/agrohydrology/ks_0.61mmh/` and `ks_2mmh/` (untracked; never `git add` that folder: its PNGs and `.cas` are not ignored) and said their boundaries were wrong. What was found: the two differ in the soil only (Ks 0.61 / 2 mm/h in the geometry, theta_i 0.2 / 0.32, theta_s 0.4 / 0.56, suction 0.1 m, TELEMAC's Travert Green-Ampt, FV scheme 5, Strickler 30, 8 h, the same hyetograph of 42.13 mm in 7 h); the coordinates are EPSG:25832 (13.117 E, 48.498 N, Lower Bavaria; the QGIS project says WGS84, which is wrong), and the mesh bottom is the Bavarian open DGM1 (tiles `803_5379`, `804_5379`, `803_5380`, `804_5380` from `download1.bayernwolke.de/a/dgm/dgm1/`, CC BY 4.0, attribution "Bayerische Vermessungsverwaltung – www.geodaten.bayern.de") interpolated to the nodes: 5 cm std, except 50 outline nodes and one 20 m pit in `ks_2mmh`; and every one of the 1,203 outline nodes was an open `5 4 4` with depth 0, also the upper edges. So the example is **merged into one**: the field (mesh outline simplified by 0.5 m), the DGM1 itself, the original `hyetograph.txt` unchanged, two soil tables (`soil-ks-0.61.csv` in the case, `soil-ks-2.csv` for step 5 of the guide), Strickler 30, a 2 m mesh (16,600 nodes against 61,300), Sadeghi instead of Travert. The research inputs are in `cases/agrohydrology/user-sources/` (gitignored), written by a reconstruction script that is not tracked (it reads the untracked models); the commit message holds its rules. **The field is no catchment**: routed over the DGM1 from its outline (the checkup's priority flood at 1 m), 78 % drains over the north edge (toward a creek), 11 % over the west edge (a steep bank) and 9 % over the southeast edge (a furrow 5 m outside); those are the three outflow lines (bins of 10 m with more than 50 m2 of exits, the edge cells alone giving 10 to 17, runs joined over gaps up to 70 m, runs under 20 m dropped; a threshold of 40 joined the southeast and north edges over the east corner, a gap of 30 left a stretch of the southeast edge to the wall), and the checkup finds 0.3 % crossing walls, below its 2 %. About 2.3 ha upslope drain onto the field (mostly along the tillage lines of the field to the southwest); the example leaves that run-on out and says so.

**What the example needed in aXqua, each pinned in `tests/test_hydrology.py`.** (1) **`hydrology.outlet_condition: overfall`**, for an outline that is the lower edge of a field rather than a divide: the outflow lines get a level 1 m below the lowest node (`hydrology.overfall_level`), so TELEMAC's `bord.f` gives them a depth of zero and the FV kernel (`cdl_fv.f`) a dry ghost: water leaves as over a brink and none comes in. Measured on the example: `free` took in up to 2.7 m3/s through the north edge 16 min after runoff began (FV free = zero gradient, `HG=H1, UG=U1`, so an inward velocity is an inflow); `normal_depth` let 1.4 m3 in through the west line, whose lowest point is a corner the field drains away from (the outlet slope came out -0.037 and fell back to 1e-4, Z(0.44 m3/s) 0.65 m above the bed); `overfall` let nothing in and closed to 3e-7, the bulk results of the two equal (runoff coefficient 0.628, peak 0.123 m3/s). The FE kernel with `overfall` also took nothing in (1e-15), only drained slower. (2) **TELEMAC's own hyetograph file** (`#HYETOGRAPH FILE`, RAINDEF 3 of `RUNOFF_GREENAMPT`) is read as TELEMAC reads it (`rainfall.telemac_hyetograph`: two lines skipped, the depth of a line falls in the interval ending at its time, the first line's depth belongs to no interval); it used to stop with a pandas `ParserError`. (3) A hydrology case shows water from 1 mm (`config.HYDROLOGY_WET_DEPTH` as `hydrodynamics.wet_depth` unless the case sets one): with the river's 1 cm the depth map of sheet flow is nearly empty. (4) The hydrology report deletes the per-processor listings, as the steady analysis does, and `axqua <case>` names `axqua submit <case> --kind hydrology` as the next step instead of HydroBayesCal.

**The fifth review (`995f050...a7ed02f`) and what it changed.** **What an outlet prescribes is one table, `core.boundaries.OUTLET_LEVELS`** (condition -> `None` no level, `curve` TELEMAC's own Z(Q), `fixed`, `rating`, `mesh` for overfall), asked through `outlet_level(condition)` (one function, also for stand-ins of a config) by the `.cli` code, the prescribed arrays, the liquid-boundaries file, `hydraulics.resolve_outflow_wse` / `outflow_wse_fn` (one implementation, which `pipeline` and `unsteady` alias), the hydrology build (which hands its mesh's level to `outflow_wse_fn(cfg, level=...)`), the config check, the validation, the OpenFOAM tailwater and the schema (`RIVER_OUTLETS`, `HYDROLOGY_OUTLETS`, default first); compare outlet kinds, never condition names: `overfall` had been missed in two hand-kept lists, and an unsteady run of a hydrology case crashed on it. That run no longer exists: a hydrology case follows the hydrograph of its inflow lines in the hydrology run, so UNSTEADY2D/3D are not configured in the mode and `unsteady._prepare_forcing` refuses it. **`hydrodynamics.wet_depth` is empty by default and `Config.wet_depth` resolves it** (1 cm, 1 mm in the mode); setting 1 mm at load had made a derived default stored state that a dump wrote out and that outlived switching the mode off. Read the property, never the field. TELEMAC's hyetograph file is a file whose first line begins with `#HYETOGRAPH` (a comment that only names a hyetograph is an ordinary table), its first data line needs only the time (`READ(UL,*) AT1`), and the test reads one line. `axqua example get <name>` looks for a name the index of the installed release lacks in the next source (main), as a release tag predates the examples added after it.

**The sixth review (`a7ed02f...832bc29`).** An example that only a later source has (the main branch, for an installed release or an older checkout) is not placed: its case file may use settings the installed aXqua cannot read, so `examples.fetch` names where it is and how to get it (update, or `--source`). **A river through the catchment (the user's order of 2026-10-30, optional).** An outflow line of a hydrology case may carry its own outlet in a column `Outlet` of the liquid-boundary layer (`core.boundaries.line_conditions`; a river case ignores it with a warning); an inflow line its discharge in `Target flow`. Every per-line consumer asks `core.boundaries.line_condition(cfg, lb)` (`lb.condition or cfg.outflow_condition`): the `.cli` code (`boundary._outflow_code(cfg, lb)`), the curve flags and curves (`hydrology.curve_lines`), the levels (`hydrology.line_levels`, a dict index -> level that `steering._prescribed_arrays` and `write_liquid_boundaries` accept besides one value), `liquid-boundaries.json` (`condition`). **TELEMAC splits liquid boundaries only where node codes change** (`front2.f`: LIHBOR/LIUBOR of neighbors), so `normal_depth` touching `overfall` (both `5 4 4`) would be ONE boundary; `boundary._split_outlets` turns two nodes between touching outflow lines with different outlets into walls, taken from the longer line (one wall node is TELEMAC's "SOLID POINT BETWEEN TWO LIQUID POINTS", found on the first real run; a lone liquid node is walled too). Real run on the V-catchment with a 0.2 m3/s river (normal_depth) and overfall field edges: 4 boundaries, the river leaves through its own line, the edges only export, volume error 1e-16. **The Green-Ampt infiltration applies to the river bed too** (105 m3 of river water in 25 min there): the docs tell the user to give the channel Ks 0 or a sealed surface. The report writes `boundary-volumes.csv`; its error is relative to rain + inflow and the runoff coefficient is (outflow - inflow) / rain since then (identical without inflow). CLI `axqua hydrology lines <case> [--set JSON]` (`hydrology/river.py`) lists the lines and writes a copy with the columns into `user-sources/hydrology/liquid-boundaries.gpkg`; the plugin's data wizard has the tab *River* for it. Checkup codes `outlet_unknown` (error), `river_without_outlet` (warning).

**Sediment transport in the hydrology mode (the user's order of 2026-10-30: strongly recommended, may be switched off).** `Config._check_hydrology` no longer refuses `morphodynamics`, and `_check_bed_change_data` asks no DoD in the mode. `hydrology.write_case` writes `gaia.cas` and couples it; `axqua hydrology sediment` (`hydrology/sediment.py`, `recommend` / `recommend_for_case`) turns a soil (texture class -> `TEXTURE_SHARES`, clay, sand, shares, d50) and the median slope of the terrain into the block: sand van Rijn 7 + 3, clay one CO class (Partheniades/Krone, GAIA's defaults 0.5 N/m2 and 1e-3 kg/m2/s, dry density 1300), loams CO (silt+clay) + NCO 0.15 mm (CO first, as GAIA's examples), gravel MPM, slope effect above 2 %, warning above 20 %. New `Morphodynamics` fields (all None/False by default, so a river GAIA file is unchanged): `suspension_formula`, `layer_thickness`, `mud_erosion_shear`, `partheniades`, `mud_concentration`, `equilibrium_inflow`, `soil`; per class `type`, `fraction`; property `suspended_classes` (CO always + NCO with `suspended_load`). **Four defects found only by running it in TELEMAC, all of which also hit the river GAIA path**: (1) `COUPLING PERIOD FOR GAIA` is no keyword of TELEMAC v9.1.1 (only TOMAWAC has one) and every coupled run stopped at it; not written any more, `coupling_period` != 1 warns. (2) With GAIA, a `PREVIOUS COMPUTATION FILE` must hold `BOTTOM` (`telemac2d_init.F`); the dry-start/pre-wet files get it only when GAIA is on (`steering._bed_for_gaia`), since TELEMAC takes the bed of the file whenever it is there. (3) The `.cli` tracer column (LITBOR) was 2 everywhere; with suspended classes (= tracers) it is 5 at inflow and 4 at outflow nodes, else suspended sediment could not leave; without tracers it stays 2. (4) With rain and tracers TELEMAC needs `VALUES OF TRACERS IN THE RAIN` (one 0 per suspended class), and the hydrology printouts add `T*`. Also: `EQUILIBRIUM INFLOW CONCENTRATION` put ~88 g/l of a cohesive class into a 0.2 m3/s river, so the recommendation leaves it off where a CO class exists (a river brings clear water; its bed load comes in at GAIA's equilibrium). **The sediment budget** (`hydrology/report.read_sediment`, `sediment_budget`, `sediment-budget.csv`, `bedload_kg` in `boundary-volumes.csv`): erosion and deposition integrated from GAIA's per-printout `EROSION FLUX` / `DEPOSITION FLUX` (summed over processors by GAIA), bed load per boundary the same way; net loss of the bed, mass out and mass left from TELEMAC's cumulated tracer balance, whose "QUANTITY CREATED BY SOURCE TERM" is erosion MINUS deposition (GAIA applies both through the tracer source); `exchange_mismatch` compares the two and warns above 5 % (printouts too sparse). GAIA's final cumulated balance needs `LT == NIT`, which a variable-dt run never reaches. **TELEMAC v9.1.1 bug, found 2026-11-02 on the agrohydrology example:** in a parallel FV run, `bilant1.f` books `DT*FLUTSOR`/`DT*FLUTENT` of `flux_trac.f` per subdomain without `P_SUM`, so each listing names only its own subdomain's tracer mass through the liquid boundaries (the 16-core run's main listing: 0 kg). `_processor_boundary_mass` adds the `<listing>_p0000N.sortie` copies of the same run (FV only; identical values are taken as already summed), so `analyze` must read before it deletes them. aXqua 1.2.0 read the main listing alone and took the missing share for deposition, and the earlier notes here ("lost mass is the deposition", "52 to 122 kg", "the GAIA fluxes gave twice the erosion") were artifacts of that: the example's first soil is 405 kg eroded, 310 kg deposited, 95.6 kg out, net and out equal to 1e-4. Checkup codes `sediment_undecided` (no block at all; `enabled: false` is a decision) and `sediment_out_of_range`. **Steering files and DAMOCLES' 72 columns**: every `.cas` writer (`write_cas`, `write_gaia_cas`, the hotstart, `threed`) ends with `steering.fit_columns`, which puts a value list that does not fit on continuation lines (each ending with `;`, inside column 72); `read_cas_values`, `steering.set_keyword` (behind `bayescal._patch_cas`/`_set_cas`, so validation and multi-flow runs) read and replace a keyword over all its lines. HydroBayesCal rewrites the first line of a keyword only, so `calibration._refuse_continued_keywords` stops a calibration of a continued keyword. **A river inflow limits the sediment setups** (measured 2026-11-01 on the V-catchment with a river, FV kernel, `hydrology/sediment.river_conflicts`, checkup error `sediment_with_river`): not the number of layers and not clay with sand as such (GAIA allows any mix; without a river every soil ran), but clear river water over an erodible bed with GAIA's erosion/deposition applied explicitly in the FV kernel (`init_trac.f`, deposition `ws x CSRATIO` of `suspension_rouse.f`). Runs: CO alone, suspended NCO <= 0.15 mm (also with CO, at 0.2 and 1 m3/s), NCO-only 0.25-0.5 mm with `equilibrium_inflow`. Stops: suspended NCO > 0.15 mm with CO (also with equilibrium) or without equilibrium (T > 1000 g/l at the plug at t = 0), > 0.5 mm also with it, and bed load with no suspended class (heap crash in `gaia_prepare_step`, any size). A lower dry-start plug is no remedy (5 cm blows up the hydraulics without GAIA). Separately, the FE kernel with an inflow line in the mode stops in DEBIMP even without GAIA (open). Plugin: `gui/sediment_wizard.py` (tabs Soil layer, Grain sizes, Recommendation, Save), button *Sediment transport wizard...* in `HydrologyDataBox`, `capability_tabs.PART_OF_HYDROLOGY`. Docs: section *Sediment transport* of the Hydrology page (OUTLINE updated). Limits stated there: no raindrop detachment, no rills, river formulas, starting values.
